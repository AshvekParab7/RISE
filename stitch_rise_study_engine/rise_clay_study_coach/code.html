Can you explain the role of ATP synthase again? I'm still a bit confused about how the proton gradient actually powers it.

psychology
RISE Coach

Absolutely. Think of ATP synthase as a microscopic, molecular turbine embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

The proton gradient means there are many more protons ($H^+$ ions) outside the membrane than inside. Because nature seeks balance, these protons desperately want to flow back across the membrane. The only way through is a channel within the ATP synthase enzyme.

As protons rush through this channel, their physical movement causes the base of the enzyme to physically spin. This mechanical rotation provides the exact physical energy needed to jam a phosphate group onto an ADP molecule, creating ATP.
